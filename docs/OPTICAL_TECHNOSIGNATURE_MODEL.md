# 🔬 High-Energy Pulsed Optical Laser Technosignature Model

## 1. Executive Summary & Physics Overview

Pulsed Optical SETI (O-SETI) searches for monochromatic, high-power optical laser pulses ($\lambda \sim 350 - 1000 \text{ nm}$) directed towards Earth.

Because interstellar extinction in the optical band is low over hundreds of parsecs, pulsed optical lasers (e.g. frequency-doubled $\text{Nd:YAG}$ at $\lambda = 532 \text{ nm}$ or fundamental $\text{Nd:YAG}$ at $\lambda = 1064 \text{ nm}$) offer extraordinary energy efficiency for interestellar communication.

A pulsed megawatt laser pulse focused by an optical primary mirror ($D \sim 10 \text{ m}$) outshines the host star by **several orders of magnitude** during nanosecond emission bursts:

$$\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\text{laser}}}{\mathcal{F}_{\text{star}}} \gg 10^3$$

## 2. Spectroscopic Identification Criteria

High-resolution optical spectrographs (such as the **Automated Planet Finder - APF** at Lick Observatory, operating at resolution $R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta \lambda} \approx 100,000$) identify pulsed optical lasers through three key spectroscopic characteristics:

1. **Ultra-Narrow Monochromatic Linewidth ($\Delta \lambda \le 0.05 \text{ \AA}$):**
   Artificial laser emissions are physically constrained by the laser cavity and quantum coherence, yielding line widths significantly narrower than thermal Doppler-broadened stellar absorption or emission lines ($\Delta \lambda_{\text{thermal}} \sim 0.2 - 1.0 \text{ \AA}$).

2. **High Peak-to-Continuum Flux Ratio ($\mathcal{F}_{\text{peak}} / \mathcal{F}_{\text{continuum}} \ge 5.0$):**
   The laser flux appears as a intense monochromatic spike rising sharply above the local stellar blackbody continuum.

3. **High Statistical Significance ($\sigma_{\text{pulse}} \ge 8.0$):**
   Signal-to-noise detection exceeding background Poisson photon noise by $\ge 8 \sigma$.
